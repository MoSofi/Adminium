An add-on's step can be about a person (from Adminium 0.3.41). An input of kind `person` is whose row of a people table the step is about: the rule's record itself where an app declares its table as people (`@record`), or the person one link of the record names (`@link:<column>`, "the job's main contact"; a column that copies a link through another, such as a job's contact copied from its deal, counts as that link), checked at the save, one hop and no more. The rule editor's sources say which tables hold people (`people`) and which columns name one (`personLinks`). The step's columns read that person's `email`, `name`, `language` (the install's own where the table keeps none), `company`, `table` and `key` (`{ person, field }`), read as the system through the app's people declaration; the address and the name go only into columns the add-on keeps personal and never into the run's log, and a record that names nobody, or somebody with no address, fails by name and writes nothing. A `record` input may name its row by a unique column (`by`, a survey by its slug) and hold the rows it may pick to some values (`where`, 1 to 3: only live surveys), in the rule editor's list and at the run. An input may carry a line under it (`help`), and a choice's options a line each, drawn as cards. The tokens `recordTable` and `recordKey` are the rule's record as other rows name it: its table's stored name and its key. A step's row with a text key nothing else fills is given a fresh id. And a table an automation runs an add-on's step on counts as `linked` for that add-on's record tabs.
